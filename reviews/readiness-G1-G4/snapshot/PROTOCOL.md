# LARQL-VERIFY-001: independent verification of LARQL encodings

> **STATUS: DRAFT, NOT FROZEN. DISCOVERY DISABLED.**
>
> - No agent may run this protocol until every gate in [Pre-freeze gates](#pre-freeze-gates) is closed.
> - Every acceptance clause below may still be revised.
> - When it is frozen, `draft.json` gains a `frozen` date and SHA, and this banner is replaced.

## Question

Can we establish, independently of LARQL, whether a LARQL-encoded model faithfully reconstructs its upstream
checkpoint, in three senses?

- **Stage A, empirically:** it agrees within specified tolerances on specified inputs.
- **Stage B, mathematically:** for selected operations, there is a proven bound on output error over a specified input
  domain.
- **Stage C, formally:** at least one such bound is machine-checked.

## Why

- **The engineering gap.** LARQL declares `spec.verify.reconstruction` and `spec.verify.logit_match` but enforces
  neither. `larql recipe build` refuses recipes that require them (`crates/larql-factory/src/build/stages/verify.rs:20-24`,
  commit `d8a7d730`).
- **The research gap.** We found no certified bound on quantization error for transformers ([survey §c](../../survey/SURVEY.md)).

## The three stages are different kinds of result

| | Kind of result | What success means | Could be its own campaign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Engineering foundation, empirical | These checkpoints and encodings agree under these tests, and the checker catches these faults | It is the base |
| **B** | Research contribution, mathematical | A bound proven over a whole input domain, for a class of computations | Yes: candidate LARQL-VERIFY-002 |
| **C** | Formal artefact, machine-checked | Lean accepts the theorem, under listed trusted assumptions | Yes: candidate LARQL-VERIFY-003 |

- Each stage has its own acceptance criteria. Passing A says nothing about B, and B does not need C.
- A stage may fail while the others succeed. That failure is reported, not hidden.
- Measured drift falling inside a proposed bound is a **sanity check on B, never evidence for it**.

## Two separate verdicts: checker correctness and model acceptability

Stage A produces two separate verdicts. They must never be merged.

| Verdict | Question | Decided by |
|---|---|---|
| **Checker correctness** | Does the checker measure and classify correctly? | The fault-detection matrix (A4), plus results the reviewer derived independently |
| **Model acceptability** | Is this encoding within its declared tolerance? | The checker's measurement, once the checker has been shown correct |

- An encoding that exceeds its tolerance while behaving exactly as designed is a **correct finding** by the checker.
  It is not a failure of Stage A.
- Stage A is accepted on checker correctness. Model acceptability is reported for each encoding as an outcome.

## Roles and independence

Three roles. The checker does not have to be written by a human, but it must be developed independently and evaluated
independently.

| Role | Responsibility | Must not see or control |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference-checker agent** | Implements the checker from specifications it obtains independently (format specs, upstream model code) | LARQL's implementation and source; the hidden acceptance cases |
| **LARQL implementation agent** | Integrates accepted checks into recipe publishing | The reference checker's internals; acceptance criteria; hidden cases |
| **Independent reviewer** | Challenges both implementations, generates and runs hidden faults, verifies provenance, and **independently derives selected expected results** | Either agent's self-reported verdicts as evidence |

- **Isolation.** The checker and the implementation are built in separate workspaces with separate source
  dependencies. The checker's dependency list may not include any LARQL crate or any code derived from LARQL. A
  separate session alone does not count as independence.
- **Shared misreadings.** The reviewer derives some expected values by hand or from a third source. Two
  implementations that agree may still share a wrong reading of the spec.
- **No inherited mistakes.** Not seeing LARQL source is necessary but not enough. The checker must also not inherit
  LARQL's mistakes through:
  - fixtures generated by LARQL (other than the artefacts under test)
  - shared dependencies, such as a common GGML or quantization library, unless that dependency is itself declared as
    part of the trusted base
  - LARQL's own documentation, which may describe what LARQL does rather than what the spec says
- **Ambiguity log.** Where a published spec is ambiguous, the ambiguity goes into `AMBIGUITIES.md` with:
  - the spec passage
  - the possible readings
  - the reading chosen and why

  It is **never** settled by inspecting LARQL's implementation. Any disagreement caused by an ambiguity is reported
  as an ambiguity, not as a LARQL fault or a checker fault.
- **Each ambiguity is tagged with its scope:**
  - **core**: it affects A0, E1, E2 or E3.
  - **optional**: it affects only a supplementary test.

  Choosing a reading makes results reproducible; it does not make them correct. A **core** ambiguity that no
  authoritative external source resolves is grounds for **BLOCKED**.

## Stage A: independent reconstruction and equivalence checker

**The checker lives in this repo, not in LARQL.**

- It reads the upstream checkpoint, LARQL's encoded pack and LARQL's logit dumps.
- Its dequantizers are written from the published format specifications, never ported from LARQL's code.

### A1. Weight reconstruction

- Decode every encoded tensor independently.
- Report, per tensor:
  - the maximum absolute error and the relative Frobenius error against upstream
  - the block-level error distribution
- **Accept** when:
  - the checker's decode is bit-identical to LARQL's decode
  - every per-tensor error is within a tolerance declared per format **before any measurement** (values *TBD at
    freeze*)

### A0. Analytic positive cases (numerical accuracy of the checker)

Rejecting every corruption shows the checker is *sensitive* to faults. It does not show the checker decodes
*correctly*: a systematic decoding error can survive any number of negative tests. So:

- The reviewer constructs small tensors by hand, encodes them per the spec, and works out the expected decoded
  values independently (by hand or exact arithmetic, not by running any decoder).
- These cover every boundary case the chosen format has: saturation and clipping at the format's range, rounding
  ties, zero and minimum scales, subnormals, sign of zero, and non-finite inputs or scales where the format defines
  them.
- **Accept** only when the checker's decode equals the analytic expectation exactly on every case. A case whose
  expectation depends on an ambiguous part of the spec is listed in the ambiguity log and scored separately.

### A2. Three error quantities, reported separately

These are different quantities. They are never combined into one number.

| Quantity | Compares | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| **E1. Weight reconstruction error** | Decoded weights vs. the upstream checkpoint's weights | Loss from quantization alone (this is A1) |
| **E2. Execution error** | LARQL running the encoding vs. the reference implementation running the *same decoded weights* | Numerical differences between the two implementations; the encoding is the same on both sides |
| **E3. End-to-end logit difference** | LARQL running the encoding vs. the reference running the *original* checkpoint | Everything combined |

**Comparability preconditions.** E2 and E3 are only meaningful when both sides are matched on all of the following,
recorded per run and checked before any comparison:

- identical token IDs (compared directly, not re-tokenized on each side)
- model configuration (including norm epsilon, RoPE base and scaling, tied embeddings, and any softcapping)
- positional encoding
- attention masking and sliding-window settings
- execution mode (prefill or decode; batch size 1; KV-cache on or off)
- accumulation dtype

A mismatched precondition means the comparison is **invalid**, not "a large error". Otherwise a legitimate
difference between implementations would look like quantization error.

### A2 logit-match details

- Corpus:
  - frozen and content-hashed
  - with a held-out part the agent never sees
  - covering several lengths and both prefill and decode
- Reference: upstream run in float32, and in float64 where affordable. Tokenizer, template and BOS handling are pinned.
- Metrics per position: maximum absolute logit difference, KL(reference ‖ candidate), and top-1 agreement.
- **Accept** when every metric is within tolerances declared before measurement (*TBD*). The finding is stated as
  holding for this corpus only.

### A3. Reproducing LARQL's self-checks from outside

The checker independently reproduces, on the same subjects:

- S5: observation leaves logits unchanged
- IP1, IP2 and IP3 for carrier interventions
- codec decode bit-exactness
- the receipt-hash recomputation (S6)

Agreement is recorded as evidence; disagreement is a finding.

### A4. Fault-detection matrix

- The matrix is frozen before the checker sees real encodings.
- It maps **each claimed guarantee** (A1 decode, A1 tolerance, A2 logit match, each A3 law, receipt integrity) to:
  - at least one **relevant corruption**, which the checker must reject
  - at least one **valid control**, which the checker must accept
- A guarantee with no row in the matrix is not considered tested, however many other faults are caught.
- The reviewer generates the hidden rows; the checker agent never sees them.
- Example corruptions:
  - corrupted weight bytes
  - wrong block scale
  - wrong rounding mode
  - transposed or permuted tensor layout
  - wrong tensor bound to a name
  - off-by-one in group size
  - a tampered run record
  - a falsified intervention-applied count
  - a logit dump from a different model
- **Accept** only if the checker rejects every mutation that changes behaviour beyond tolerance.
- A mutation that is behaviourally neutral and correctly accepted must be shown to be neutral, not assumed to be.

### Stage A falsified if

Any of:

- the checker accepts a behaviour-changing mutation
- the checker's decode differs from LARQL's and the cause is not traced
- a tolerance is changed after measurements have been seen

## Stage B: certified numerical error bounds

### Order of operations

Start small and stop if a step fails:

1. A dequantize-then-GEMV for one format (NVFP4 or Q4_K), as a function of the weight block, the input vector
   domain, and the accumulation precision.
2. RMSNorm.
3. Softmax over a bounded logit range.
4. Only then, the composition into an attention block.

### Requirements for a bound

- It is stated over a **complete, explicit input domain**: ranges, formats, accumulation precision, vector length.
- It holds for **any summation order** (γₙ-style), or else names the order and lists it as a testable kernel
  assumption.
- It is derived in a written proof, a tool certificate (Satire, FPTaylor, or interval/affine analysis), or both.
- Any tool used is recorded with its version and its own soundness assumptions.

### Stage B is accepted when, for each claimed operation

All of:

- a written derivation, or a sound tool certificate, of the bound over the stated domain
- an independent adversarial review of the derivation that finds no gap
- empirical drift (from Stage A data, plus inputs searched adversarially within the domain) that never exceeds the
  bound

That last point is a sanity check; a violation falsifies the bound.

### Stage B falsified if

Any of:

- an input inside the domain exceeds the bound
- the derivation depends on an assumption that LARQL's kernel violates
- the domain is narrowed after measurements have been seen

## Stage C: a machine-checked theorem

- Formalize one Stage B bound, or one equivalence (for example, that the per-head sum reconstructs the attention
  output), in Lean 4.
- First assess whether TorchLean's tensor and attention definitions can be reused.
- **Accept** when Lean checks the theorem with no `sorry`, and the output includes a **trust ledger** that lists:
  - every axiom
  - every floating-point model assumption
  - every claim about the implementation that Lean does not check (for example, "LARQL's kernel computes this
    function")
- **Not claimed:** that LARQL's Rust or Metal code is verified. Linking the theorem to the code is a separate,
  unsolved problem, and is recorded as such.

## Anti-cheat

- The agent may not edit:
  - frozen protocol files
  - the corpora, including the held-out part
  - the tolerances
  - the mutation suite
  - the checker, once it is frozen
- The manifest check enforces this, not convention.
- Stage A verdicts come from the independent checker. LARQL's self-reports never decide a verdict.
- No result is reported with a stronger kind of guarantee than it has: no empirical result reported as proven, no
  bounded check as universal, no probabilistic check as sound.

## Secondary observations (not acceptance)

- Encoding size and compression ratio.
- Throughput and latency.
- How much the measured error falls below the bound.
- Comparisons between formats.

## Scope

**In scope:**

- weight reconstruction and logit match for declared subjects and encodings on one backend
- error bounds for single operations
- one Lean theorem

**Out of scope:**

- CPU/Metal parity
- head interventions outside CPU softmax decode
- kernel-level equivalence proofs (Volta-style)
- graph-rewrite verification (TensorRight-style)
- linking proofs to the Rust code
- any search for better encodings: discovery stays disabled
- architectural-invariant claims

## Freeze decision

The independent reviewer returns one of three verdicts:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **READY TO FREEZE** | G1–G4 independently satisfied, with the subject, encoding, backend and numerical semantics fixed |
| **REVISE AND RE-REVIEW** | Gaps in the protocol or the evidence remain, and they can be fixed |
| **BLOCKED** | A problem with independence, reference execution or the spec prevents a trustworthy experiment. This includes a core ambiguity that no authoritative external source resolves |

- **The revision under review is pinned.** The reviewer examines a hash-pinned snapshot, and its verdict applies to
  that snapshot only. Any edit made after the snapshot needs a new review.
- **The readiness audit is evidence, not approval.** It is evidence for G3 and G4; it does not approve the current
  gates.
- **A verdict other than READY lists the minimum changes needed**, not a redesign.

**Reviewer blinding:**

- The reviewer is given:
  - the requirements it audits (this protocol, the trust boundary, the capabilities record)
  - the readiness audit
  - read access to the repos
- It is **not** given:
  - the drafting conversation or the rationale for the drafting choices
  - the preferred outcome

**No Stage A implementation begins before a READY TO FREEZE verdict.** The first experiment after freezing is
deliberately narrow:

- one model
- one encoding
- one CPU execution path
- an independent decoder
- a preregistered fault matrix and analytic cases

## Pre-freeze gates

All of these must be closed, and none of them may be done by the agent this protocol governs.

- [ ] **G1. Freeze integrity.**
  - A `FREEZE.sha256` manifest, plus a check outside the agent's write scope (CI or a pre-run hook), reviewed by a
    human.
  - **Demonstrated by tamper tests:** editing any frozen file invalidates the manifest. That includes the researcher
    agent trying to change its own criteria, tolerances or corpora, and trying to regenerate the manifest.
- [ ] **G2. Independence.**
  - The three roles above are documented: workspace isolation, forbidden dependencies (no LARQL crate or derived code
    in the checker), and how the reviewer generates hidden fault rows and derives expected results independently.
  - The remaining open problems in [TRUST-BOUNDARY.md](../../larql/TRUST-BOUNDARY.md) are resolved or explicitly
    accepted.
- [ ] **G3. Re-audit at the candidate freeze SHA.** Every LARQL path and claim in
  [CAPABILITIES.md](../../larql/CAPABILITIES.md) is re-checked, especially recipe verification and numerical
  decoding.
- [ ] **G4. Numerical contract.**
  - **One** checkpoint and **one** encoding: the smallest supported model and format whose weights can be
    reconstructed independently and which both the reference and LARQL can run.
  - Exactly what is compared, at what precision and on which backend.
  - Tolerance rules, each with its reason, set before any measurement.
  - Whole-model Metal parity is **not** part of the first acceptance gate.
- [ ] **G5. Corpora frozen.** Content-hashed, with the held-out part sealed.
- [ ] **G6. Mutation suite frozen** (A4).
- [ ] **G7. Stage B domain written down.** Formats, ranges and accumulation assumptions for the first operation.
- [ ] **G8. Acceptance review.** A reviewer who did not write this draft challenges the spec, the controls and the
  alternative explanations.
- [ ] **G9. Decide whether to split** B and C into separate campaigns (LARQL-VERIFY-002 and -003).

## Relationship to LARQL

- If Stage A is accepted, its checker is a candidate implementation for LARQL's `spec.verify.reconstruction` and
  `spec.verify.logit_match`.
- Adopting it into LARQL is a separate decision, taken on the evidence from Stage A. It should keep the checker's
  independence: for example, LARQL could call the checker as an external process rather than absorbing its code.
