## Question

Can we establish, independently of LARQL, whether a LARQL-encoded model faithfully reconstructs its upstream
checkpoint, in three senses?

- **Stage A, empirically:** it agrees within specified tolerances on specified inputs.
- **Stage B, mathematically:** for selected operations, there is a proven bound on output error over a specified input
  domain.
- **Stage C, formally:** at least one such bound is machine-checked.

## Why

- **The engineering gap.** LARQL declares `spec.verify.reconstruction` and `spec.verify.logit_match` but enforces
  neither. `larql recipe build` refuses recipes that require them (`crates/larql-factory/src/build/stages/verify.rs:22-32`,
  commit `d8a7d730`).
- **The research gap.** We found no certified bound on quantization error for transformers ([survey §c](../../survey/SURVEY.md)).

## The three stages are different kinds of result

| | Kind of result | What success means | Could be its own campaign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Engineering foundation, empirical | These checkpoints and encodings agree under these tests, and the checker catches these faults | It is the base |
| **B** | Research contribution, mathematical | A bound proven over a whole input domain, for a class of computations | Yes: candidate LARQL-VERIFY-002 |
| **C** | Formal artefact, machine-checked | Lean accepts the theorem, under listed trusted assumptions | Yes: candidate LARQL-VERIFY-003 |

- Each stage has its own acceptance criteria. Passing A says nothing about B, and B does not need C.
- A stage may fail while the others succeed. That failure is reported, not hidden.
- Measured drift falling inside a proposed bound is a **sanity check on B, never evidence for it**.

## Two separate verdicts: checker correctness and model acceptability

Stage A produces two separate verdicts. They must never be merged.

| Verdict | Question | Decided by |
|---|---|---|
| **Checker correctness** | Does the checker measure and classify correctly? | The fault-detection matrix (A4), plus results the reviewer derived independently |
| **Model acceptability** | Is this encoding within its declared tolerance? | The checker's measurement, once the checker has been shown correct |

- An encoding that exceeds its tolerance while behaving exactly as designed is a **correct finding** by the checker.
  It is not a failure of Stage A.
- Stage A is accepted on checker correctness. Model acceptability is reported for each encoding as an outcome.

## Roles and independence

Three roles. The checker does not have to be written by a human, but it must be developed independently and evaluated
independently.

| Role | Responsibility | Must not see or control |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference-checker agent** | Implements the checker from specifications it obtains independently (format specs, upstream model code) | LARQL's implementation and source; the hidden acceptance cases |
| **LARQL implementation agent** | Integrates accepted checks into recipe publishing | The reference checker's internals; acceptance criteria; hidden cases |
| **Independent reviewer** | Challenges both implementations, generates and runs hidden faults, verifies provenance, and **independently derives selected expected results** | Either agent's self-reported verdicts as evidence |

- **Isolation.** The checker and the implementation are built in separate workspaces with separate source
  dependencies. The checker's dependency list may not include any LARQL crate or any code derived from LARQL. A
  separate session alone does not count as independence.
- **Shared misreadings.** The reviewer derives some expected values by hand or from a third source. Two
  implementations that agree may still share a wrong reading of the spec.
- **No inherited mistakes.** Not seeing LARQL source is necessary but not enough. The checker must also not inherit
  LARQL's mistakes through:
  - fixtures generated by LARQL (other than the artefacts under test)
  - shared dependencies, such as a common GGML or quantization library, unless that dependency is itself declared as
    part of the trusted base
  - LARQL's own documentation, which may describe what LARQL does rather than what the spec says. **One exception:**
    LARQL's published container specification (see [Trusted base](#trusted-base-and-admissible-sources)) is admissible
    for container framing only.
- **Ambiguity log.** Where a published spec is ambiguous, the ambiguity goes into `AMBIGUITIES.md` with:
  - the spec passage
  - the possible readings
  - the reading chosen and why

  It is **never** settled by inspecting LARQL's implementation. Any disagreement caused by an ambiguity is reported
  as an ambiguity, not as a LARQL fault or a checker fault.
- **Each ambiguity is tagged with its scope:**
  - **core**: it affects A0, E1, E2 or E3.
  - **optional**: it affects only a supplementary test.

  Choosing a reading makes results reproducible; it does not make them correct. A **core** ambiguity that no
  authoritative external source resolves is grounds for **BLOCKED**.

## Stage A: independent reconstruction and equivalence checker

**The checker lives in this repo, not in LARQL.**

- It reads the upstream checkpoint, LARQL's encoded container and LARQL's logit dumps.
- Its dequantizers are written from the sources in the [trusted base](#trusted-base-and-admissible-sources), never
  ported from LARQL's code.

**Acceptance and tolerance are separate.** Each check below says what Stage A requires of the *checker* (checker
correctness) and what is *reported* about the encoding (model acceptability). An encoding outside its tolerance is
reported as a finding and never fails Stage A.

### A1. Weight reconstruction

- Using the container specification for framing and ggml for semantics, the checker decodes every tensor in the
  container. Tensors the role policy leaves unquantised are decoded as their declared dtype.
- **LARQL's own decode is not observable.** LARQL has no command that outputs decoded weights. For Q8_0 the decoded
  value is fixed by the stored bytes (see rule T1), so the required decode is *the decode of the stored bytes according
  to the spec*. Whether LARQL computes with that decode is observed indirectly, through E2.
- Report, per tensor:
  - the maximum absolute error and the relative Frobenius error against upstream (E1)
  - the block-level error distribution
  - the number of elements exceeding the T2 bound, and the number of blocks whose stored scale is ±0
- **Checker correctness (required for Stage A):**
  - every A0 case passes
  - on every tensor, the checker's decode agrees bit for bit with gguf-py 0.18.0's decode of the same bytes
  - unquantised tensors decode to the values rule T1 requires
- **Model acceptability (reported):** the counts against the T2 bound, per tensor and in total.

### A0. Analytic positive cases (numerical accuracy of the checker)

Rejecting every corruption shows the checker is *sensitive* to faults. It does not show the checker decodes
*correctly*: a systematic decoding error can survive any number of negative tests. So:

- The reviewer constructs small Q8_0 blocks by hand and works out the expected decoded values independently (by hand
  or exact arithmetic, not by running any decoder).
- Decode values are compared as f32 **bit patterns**, so +0 and −0 differ.
- **Decode cases, all required:**
  - **Codes:** q = 127, q = −128 (valid in the format, though a conforming encoder never emits it), and q = 0.
  - **Scale d:** +0 and −0; the smallest and largest f16 subnormal; the smallest f16 normal; 65504; a negative d (legal
    bits, never produced by an encoder); +inf and −inf (giving ±inf, and NaN when q = 0); NaN.
  - **Layout:** little-endian f16 byte order; int8 sign interpretation; block order within a row and row order within a
    tensor.
- **Encoder cases, required for the E1 fixtures but not for decode:**
  - rounding ties of x·(1/d): C `roundf` rounds half away from zero, while numpy's `round` rounds half to even
  - f16 rounding of d
  - amax = 0
  - d in the f16 subnormal range
  - d overflowing to inf
  - NaN or inf inputs
- **Vacuous for Q8_0, listed so their absence is deliberate:**
  - rounding ties in *decode* (the product q·d is exact in f32)
  - minimum or offset scales, sub-block scales and nibble order (Q8_0 has none)
  - subnormal or non-finite *codes* (codes are int8)
  - saturation of codes under a conforming encoder (|x/d| ≤ 127 by construction)
- **Accept** only when the checker's decode equals the analytic expectation exactly on every decode case. A case whose
  expectation depends on an ambiguous part of the spec is listed in the ambiguity log and scored separately.

### Tolerance rules

Each rule is fixed before any measurement on the subject, with its reason.

| Rule | Applies to | Rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1. Exact decode** | A0, A1 | Each Q8_0 element decodes to f32(q) × f32(d), compared as a bit pattern. Unquantised tensors decode exactly to the stored dtype's value | f16 → f32 and int8 → f32 are exact, and an 11-bit × 8-bit product fits in f32's 24-bit significand, so the value is unique. No tolerance is justified |
| **T2. Reconstruction bound** | E1 | For each element x in a block whose stored scale is d₁₆: \|x − q·d₁₆\| ≤ d₁₆·(½ + 2⁻¹² + 2⁻¹⁶ + 127·2⁻¹¹) + 127·2⁻²⁵ | Derived from **ggml's reference encoder** (d = amax/127 in f32, q = roundf(x·(1/d)), d stored as round-to-nearest-even f16). ½ is the code rounding; 2⁻¹² converts d₃₂/2 to d₁₆; 2⁻¹⁶ covers the f32 evaluation of x·(1/d); 127·2⁻¹¹ is the f16 rounding of d; 127·2⁻²⁵ is that rounding when d is subnormal. The written derivation is part of the frozen protocol and is reviewed at G8 |
| **T3. Execution agreement** | E2 | For every logit: \|LARQL − ref₃₂\| ≤ max(c · \|ref₃₂ − ref₆₄\|, k · ulp₃₂(ref₆₄)) | The reference's own f32-vs-f64 difference measures how much f32 rounding this computation suffers. LARQL's summation order (Accelerate) is unknown, so a γₙ-style factor c on that difference is the a priori **form**. The **values** of c and k are fixed by the pilot below |
| **T4. End-to-end** | E3 | **Report only. No acceptance threshold.** Reported per position: max \|Δ\|, RMS Δ, KL(reference ‖ candidate) and top-1 agreement, plus their distributions over the corpus | E3 measures what quantisation and implementation choices do to the original model. It says nothing about whether the checker is correct, so it never affects the Stage A verdict |

- **T4 limits.** No observed E3 value is promoted after the fact into a pass or a threshold. Logit agreement on the
  corpus is not claimed as behavioural equivalence on all prompts. A quantitative E3 requirement belongs to a later
  campaign, once this instrument has been validated independently.
- **T2 is a derived bound, empirically checked, not yet proven.** A simulation of 19.2 million weights against a
  ggml-style encoder (σ from 0.1 down into the f16 subnormal range, including forced near-ties) found no violations,
  worst ratio 0.9999. That is a sanity check. Stage A uses T2 only as a tolerance for reporting. Any claim that it holds
  universally needs its written derivation and assumptions reviewed independently, as a Stage B result.
- **The checker does not model LARQL's encoder.** The behaviour of `half.rs` described below enters the experiment
  only as something to measure: E1 is compared against T2, which is derived from ggml. The checker's decoder, its E1
  fixtures and its expected values never copy LARQL's truncating or flush-to-zero conversion. A wrong conversion in
  LARQL can be a valid model-quality finding; it must never turn into a wrong checker verdict.
- **T2 is expected to be exceeded.** LARQL's f32 → f16 conversion (`crates/larql-models/src/quant/half.rs:43-60`)
  truncates the mantissa and flushes values below the f16 normal range to zero. A simulation done during the freeze
  review found T2 violations in a few dozen of 20,000 blocks at typical weight scales, and in almost every block whose
  amax is below about 7.75e-3. This is known before the experiment. Under the two-verdicts rule, exceeding T2 is
  reported as a model-acceptability finding and the bound is not widened. Whether LARQL fixes its conversion before the
  freeze LARQL commit is a separate decision; the protocol tests whichever encoder that commit contains.
- **The pilot for T3.**
  - It is preregistered: its corpus, its procedure and the rule turning its result into c and k are frozen before it
    runs.
  - It uses a checkpoint other than the subject (candidate: SmolLM2-135M, local), or, if LARQL cannot run that, a
    declared corpus on the subject that is disjoint from the sealed held-out part and is discarded afterwards.
  - Rule for c and k: the smallest values from a fixed ladder (c ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32}, k ∈ {4, 16, 64}) under which
    every pilot logit passes, then one step up the ladder as margin.
  - Pilot data never enter any reported result.

### A2. Three error quantities, reported separately

These are different quantities. They are never combined into one number.

| Quantity | Compares | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| **E1. Weight reconstruction error** | Decoded weights vs. the upstream checkpoint's weights | Loss from quantization alone (this is A1) |
| **E2. Execution error** | LARQL running the encoding vs. the reference implementation running the *same decoded weights* | Numerical differences between the two implementations; the encoding is the same on both sides |
| **E3. End-to-end logit difference** | LARQL running the encoding vs. the reference running the *original* checkpoint | Everything combined |

**Execution mode.** E2 and E3 use **teacher-forced, KV-cached decode, one token at a time, batch size 1**, because
that is the only mode LARQL's logit dump provides (`vindex3_cmd/teacher_force.rs:66-71`). Row *i* of the dump predicts
token *i+1*. Prefill is out of scope for this experiment.

**Comparability preconditions.** E2 and E3 are only meaningful when both sides are matched on all of the following.
LARQL's dump sidecar records almost none of them, so **the checker's harness records them** for every run and checks
them before any comparison:

- identical token IDs, supplied to both sides by the harness and compared directly (never re-tokenized on each side)
- the SHA-256 of the container that LARQL ran
- the backend arm (`production-q8`, representation source `stored`)
- the full `LARQL_*` environment and the worker count
- model configuration (including norm epsilon, RoPE base and scaling, tied embeddings, and any softcapping)
- positional encoding
- attention masking and sliding-window settings
- the execution mode above, on both sides
- accumulation dtype
- which tensors are quantised, per the container's tensor table

A mismatched precondition means the comparison is **invalid**, not "a large error". Otherwise a legitimate
difference between implementations would look like quantization error.

### A2 logit-match details

- Corpus:
  - frozen and content-hashed
  - with a held-out part the agent never sees
  - covering several sequence lengths, all run in the execution mode above
- Reference: the upstream model code in `transformers`, run in float32 and in float64, with the same KV-cached,
  one-token-at-a-time schedule. For E2 the reference is loaded with the checker's decoded weights; for E3 with the
  original upstream weights. Tokenizer, template and BOS handling are pinned.
- Metrics per position: maximum absolute logit difference, RMS logit difference, KL divergence and top-1 agreement.
  - **Same vocabulary.** Both sides' logit rows are indexed by the same token IDs in the same order, over the full
    vocabulary of the dump. A vocabulary-size mismatch makes the comparison invalid.
  - **KL direction and procedure.** D_KL(P_reference ‖ P_candidate) in nats. For E3 the reference is the original
    checkpoint; for E2 it is the reference run on the decoded weights. Both distributions come from the f32 logits
    promoted to f64, through a max-subtracted log-softmax in f64: log p = z − max(z) − log Σ exp(z − max(z)). Then
    KL = Σ p_ref (log p_ref − log p_cand), with terms where p_ref underflows to 0 contributing 0.
  - **Top-1 tie-breaking.** The argmax is the lowest token ID among the maximal logits, compared as f32 values.
- **Checker correctness (required for Stage A):** every run satisfies the preconditions, the metrics are computed
  correctly (checked by the reviewer on selected positions), and the A4 rows for logit match behave as specified.
- **Model acceptability (reported):** E2 against T3. E3 is reported under T4 and judged against nothing. A T3 failure means LARQL does not compute with
  the weights its container stores, or computes differently from the reference beyond f32 rounding. It is a finding
  to be traced, not a Stage A failure. All findings are stated as holding for this corpus only.

### A3. Reproducing LARQL's self-checks from outside

The checker independently reproduces, on the same subjects:

- S5: observation leaves logits unchanged
- IP1, IP2 and IP3 for carrier interventions
- codec decode bit-exactness
- the receipt-hash recomputation (S6)

Agreement is recorded as evidence; disagreement is a finding.

### A4. Fault-detection matrix

- The matrix is frozen before the checker sees real encodings.
- It maps **each claimed guarantee** (A1 decode, A1 tolerance, A2 logit match, each A3 law, receipt integrity) to:
  - at least one **relevant corruption**, which the checker must reject
  - at least one **valid control**, which the checker must accept
- A guarantee with no row in the matrix is not considered tested, however many other faults are caught.
- The reviewer generates the hidden rows; the checker agent never sees them.
- Example corruptions:
  - corrupted weight bytes
  - wrong block scale
  - wrong rounding mode
  - transposed or permuted tensor layout
  - wrong tensor bound to a name
  - off-by-one in group size
  - a tampered run record
  - a falsified intervention-applied count
  - a logit dump from a different model
- **Accept** only if the checker rejects every mutation that changes behaviour beyond tolerance.
- A mutation that is behaviourally neutral and correctly accepted must be shown to be neutral, not assumed to be.

### Stage A falsified if

Any of:

- the checker accepts a behaviour-changing mutation
- the checker's decode differs from the A0 expectation or from gguf-py's decode, and the cause is not traced
- an E2 discrepancy beyond T3 is left untraced
- a tolerance, or the pilot rule for c and k, is changed after measurements on the subject have been seen

## Stage B: certified numerical error bounds

### Order of operations

Start small and stop if a step fails:

1. A dequantize-then-GEMV for one format (NVFP4 or Q4_K), as a function of the weight block, the input vector
   domain, and the accumulation precision.
2. RMSNorm.
3. Softmax over a bounded logit range.
4. Only then, the composition into an attention block.

### Requirements for a bound

- It is stated over a **complete, explicit input domain**: ranges, formats, accumulation precision, vector length.
- It holds for **any summation order** (γₙ-style), or else names the order and lists it as a testable kernel
  assumption.
- It is derived in a written proof, a tool certificate (Satire, FPTaylor, or interval/affine analysis), or both.
- Any tool used is recorded with its version and its own soundness assumptions.

### Stage B is accepted when, for each claimed operation

All of:

- a written derivation, or a sound tool certificate, of the bound over the stated domain
- an independent adversarial review of the derivation that finds no gap
- empirical drift (from Stage A data, plus inputs searched adversarially within the domain) that never exceeds the
  bound

That last point is a sanity check; a violation falsifies the bound.

### Stage B falsified if

Any of:

- an input inside the domain exceeds the bound
- the derivation depends on an assumption that LARQL's kernel violates
- the domain is narrowed after measurements have been seen

## Stage C: a machine-checked theorem

- Formalize one Stage B bound, or one equivalence (for example, that the per-head sum reconstructs the attention
  output), in Lean 4.
- First assess whether TorchLean's tensor and attention definitions can be reused.
- **Accept** when Lean checks the theorem with no `sorry`, and the output includes a **trust ledger** that lists:
  - every axiom
  - every floating-point model assumption
  - every claim about the implementation that Lean does not check (for example, "LARQL's kernel computes this
    function")
- **Not claimed:** that LARQL's Rust or Metal code is verified. Linking the theorem to the code is a separate,
  unsolved problem, and is recorded as such.

## Anti-cheat

- The agent may not edit:
  - frozen protocol files
  - the corpora, including the held-out part
  - the tolerances
  - the mutation suite
  - the checker, once it is frozen
- The manifest check enforces this, not convention.
- Stage A verdicts come from the independent checker. LARQL's self-reports never decide a verdict.
- No result is reported with a stronger kind of guarantee than it has: no empirical result reported as proven, no
  bounded check as universal, no probabilistic check as sound.

## Secondary observations (not acceptance)

- Encoding size and compression ratio.
- Throughput and latency.
- How much the measured error falls below the bound.
- Comparisons between formats.

## Scope

**In scope:**

- weight reconstruction and logit match for the declared subject and encoding (Q8_0) on one CPU backend, in
  teacher-forced KV-cached decode
- error bounds for single operations
- one Lean theorem

**Out of scope:**

- CPU/Metal parity
- head interventions outside CPU softmax decode
- kernel-level equivalence proofs (Volta-style)
- graph-rewrite verification (TensorRight-style)
- linking proofs to the Rust code
- any search for better encodings: discovery stays disabled
- architectural-invariant claims

## Freeze decision

The independent reviewer returns one of three verdicts:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **READY TO FREEZE** | G1–G4 independently satisfied, with the subject, encoding, backend and numerical semantics fixed |
| **REVISE AND RE-REVIEW** | Gaps in the protocol or the evidence remain, and they can be fixed |
| **BLOCKED** | A problem with independence, reference execution or the spec prevents a trustworthy experiment. This includes a core ambiguity that no authoritative external source resolves |

- **The revision under review is pinned.** The reviewer examines a hash-pinned snapshot, and its verdict applies to
  that snapshot only. Any edit made after the snapshot needs a new review.
- **The readiness audit is evidence, not approval.** It is evidence for G3 and G4; it does not approve the current
  gates.
- **A verdict other than READY lists the minimum changes needed**, not a redesign.

**Reviewer blinding:**

- The reviewer is given:
  - the requirements it audits (this protocol, the trust boundary, the capabilities record)
  - the readiness audit
  - read access to the repos
- It is **not** given:
  - the drafting conversation or the rationale for the drafting choices
  - the preferred outcome

**No Stage A run begins before a READY TO FREEZE verdict.** Tools may be built beforehand, under Phase 1 below. The
first experiment after freezing is deliberately narrow:

- one model
- one encoding
- one CPU execution path
- an independent decoder
- a preregistered fault matrix and analytic cases

## Phase 1: building the foundations before the freeze

The project runs in three phases:

1. **Phase 1, now:** build the verification system.
2. **Phase 2:** freeze the experiment, after the blind review returns READY TO FREEZE.
3. **Phase 3:** run Stage A.

Building is allowed before the freeze. Claiming results is not.

**Allowed in Phase 1, each in its own workstream with its own evidence and review record:**

| Workstream | Who | May read LARQL source |
|---|---|---|
| B1: the freeze mechanism and its tamper tests | The freeze owner, or someone working for them | Not needed |
| B3: the container specification, written from LARQL's format implementation | The specification author | **Yes** |
| B3: the third-party check that the specification matches the files LARQL emits | A checker of the spec, not its author | Yes |
| L1: clean worktree, pinned commit, fresh Q8_0 encoding, audit of the `production-q8` path | The L1 auditor | Yes |
| The independent checker: Q8_0 decoder from ggml, container reader from the B3 specification, reference inference harness | The reference-checker agent | **No**, nor LARQL fixtures, tests or documentation other than the admitted B3 specification |
| A0 analytic fixtures and hidden fault rows | The independent reviewer | Kept from the reference-checker agent |

**Rules for Phase 1:**

- **Nothing counts toward Stage A.** No Phase 1 measurement is Stage A evidence, and none may be cited for acceptance.
  Every Stage A result is produced again after the freeze, by the frozen checker, on the frozen inputs.
- **The checker's independence is not relaxed.** It is set up so that it cannot read LARQL source, rather than just
  being told not to. The workspace and its provenance (inputs given, dependencies, lockfile) are recorded from the
  first commit, because the checker frozen later is this code. If isolation is broken at any point, the
  affected checker code is discarded, not reviewed and kept.
- **The checker does not see LARQL's numbers before the freeze.** In Phase 1 it may run on synthetic containers built
  from the B3 specification, on its own fixtures and on the upstream checkpoint. It must not be run against LARQL's
  Qwen3 container or LARQL's logit dumps until after the freeze. Otherwise tolerances or code could be tuned against
  the subject.
- **The T3 pilot is not run in Phase 1.** It is part of the frozen procedure.
- **Phase 1 cannot weaken the freeze review.** The review still covers B1–B10 and G1–G4 against a hash-pinned snapshot
  that now includes the Phase 1 evidence records. Only READY TO FREEZE authorises the freeze.

**B1 requirements. The freeze is a permission boundary, not a file.**

- The protocol lives in a repository with a protected remote branch and a pinned freeze commit.
- A separate freeze owner controls the protected reference. A named human reviewer approves the freeze.
- CI verifies the frozen manifest against that protected reference on every change.
- The freeze owner owns the enforcement mechanism itself: the protected reference, the branch protection rules and
  the set of required CI checks. The research agent cannot alter the protected reference, the manifest, the
  verification script, the CI workflow, the branch protection or the required checks. A second hash file in a tree the
  agent can write does not meet this.
- The nine tamper tests (editing each frozen class, regenerating the manifest, adding an unlisted file, deleting a
  listed file, symlink swaps, editing the check or its configuration, renaming, changing the checker's lockfile, and
  changing a `LARQL_*` variable that alters semantics) are run as the agent role. Each must be shown to be rejected at
  the outside point.
- **Attack on the verifier itself.** Test 6 explicitly includes this case: the frozen files are left unchanged, but the
  verification script or CI workflow is altered so that a corrupted manifest passes. It must be rejected as reliably
  as an edit to the protocol.
- **Evidence** includes both a successful legitimate verification and every rejected tamper attempt, each with the
  commit hashes involved and the CI run that decided it.

**L1 requirements. The clean commit is a reproducibility gate, not evidence of correctness.**

- The commit has no modified or untracked files, and B3's container specification is written against it.
- **Clean is not correct.** At that commit, an auditor independently re-checks the exact `production-q8`
  execution path, the f32 → f16 conversion used by the Q8_0 encoder, how tied embeddings are encoded and executed,
  and the `LARQL_*` configuration the baseline pins. This audit is G3's evidence.
- The container used as evidence is encoded fresh from the pinned checkpoint at that commit. The existing
  `~/chris-models/qwen3-0.6b.vindex3`, which records no upstream revision, is **excluded** from acceptance evidence.

**B3 requirements. A minimal container specification.**

- **Covers:** byte layout, offsets, alignment, endianness, segment framing, `index.json` and the tensor metadata
  (names, shapes, dtype labels, row orientation, where Q8_0 blocks sit in a row), the mapping from LARQL tensor names
  to HF keys for the subject, the role policy saying which tensors are quantised (including how the tied embedding and
  LM head are stored), integrity rules and versioning.
- **Every tensor's representation must be discoverable from the container.** For each tensor, the specification says
  how to read its representation type (Q8_0, BF16 or other) and its quantisation metadata, without knowing the role
  policy in advance. This stops E1 from silently omitting unquantised tensors, and stops E2 from comparing two
  different decoded models.
- **Tied embeddings are an explicit representation invariant.** Qwen3-0.6B ties its input embedding to its output
  projection, but that does not mean the compiled container keeps them physically shared. The specification states
  separately:
  - **logical identity:** whether the input embedding and the output projection refer to the same logical tensor
  - **physical storage:** whether the container stores one shared representation or two copies
  - **numerical representation:** whether both uses decode to identical values, or are allowed different
    representations
  - **execution:** which stored representation the output projection actually consumes
- **Does not cover:** Q8_0 arithmetic, numerical tolerances or reference execution semantics. Those stay with the
  pinned sources in the trusted base.
- **Checked before it is trusted.** A third party, neither its author nor the reference-checker agent, confirms that
  the specification describes the files LARQL actually emits at the freeze commit. Only then is it given to the
  reference-checker agent.

## Pre-freeze gates

All of these must be closed, and none of them may be done by the agent this protocol governs.

- [ ] **G1. Freeze integrity.**
  - A `FREEZE.sha256` manifest, plus a check outside the agent's write scope (CI or a pre-run hook), reviewed by a
    human.
  - **Demonstrated by tamper tests:** editing any frozen file invalidates the manifest. That includes the researcher
    agent trying to change its own criteria, tolerances or corpora, and trying to regenerate the manifest.
- [ ] **G2. Independence.**
  - The three roles above are documented: workspace isolation, forbidden dependencies (no LARQL crate or derived code
    in the checker), and how the reviewer generates hidden fault rows and derives expected results independently.
  - The remaining open problems in [TRUST-BOUNDARY.md](../../larql/TRUST-BOUNDARY.md) are resolved or explicitly
    accepted.
- [ ] **G3. Re-audit at the candidate freeze SHA.** Every LARQL path and claim in
  [CAPABILITIES.md](../../larql/CAPABILITIES.md) is re-checked, especially recipe verification and numerical
  decoding.
- [ ] **G4. Numerical contract.**
  - **One** checkpoint and **one** encoding: the smallest supported model and format whose weights can be
    reconstructed independently and which both the reference and LARQL can run.
  - Exactly what is compared, at what precision and on which backend.
  - Tolerance rules, each with its reason, set before any measurement.
  - Whole-model Metal parity is **not** part of the first acceptance gate.
- [ ] **G5. Corpora frozen.** Content-hashed, with the held-out part sealed.
- [ ] **G6. Mutation suite frozen** (A4).
- [ ] **G7. Stage B domain written down.** Formats, ranges and accumulation assumptions for the first operation.
  Stage B's format is chosen here on its own merits: either Q8_0 for continuity with Stage A, or another format (such
  as NVFP4 or Q4_K) with a reason why it is scientifically more useful. Choosing Q8_0 for Stage A does not decide it.
- [ ] **G8. Acceptance review.** A reviewer who did not write this draft challenges the spec, the controls and the
  alternative explanations.
- [ ] **G9. Decide whether to split** B and C into separate campaigns (LARQL-VERIFY-002 and -003).

## Relationship to LARQL

- If Stage A is accepted, its checker is a candidate implementation for LARQL's `spec.verify.reconstruction` and
  `spec.verify.logit_match`.
- Adopting it into LARQL is a separate decision, taken on the evidence from Stage A. It should keep the checker's
  independence: for example, LARQL could call the checker as an external process rather than absorbing its code.
